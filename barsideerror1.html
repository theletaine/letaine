Barside Errors I

Shrimp cocktail. Snake wine. An old-fashioned on rocks. (The bartender sourced pebbles from the fish tank.) These are some of the unconventional offerings the bar car provides.
The Letaine offers many amenities, among which are water, food, shelter, and booze. And in all earnestness, what else could a passenger ask for? These basic needs fulfill the demands of our physical and mental well-being. Your drink order may come out incorrect; however, that’s a feature of an ‘avant-garde’ bar. The staff whispers about that being a slapdash branding decision. Customers claim it isn’t that bad if you get the item struck from the bill. The true oddballs find a thrill in the roulette. The bartender has yet to be fired, but the conductor is known to take on charity cases. As they say, beggars can’t be choosers, and apparently, clients can’t choose either. Here’s a tip: throw a big enough fuss, and you might get your tab written off.
—Testimonial from G.

A short, crisp rap of a knock announces the arrival of the post. With a hiss and a clunk, the mailperson opens the door to the lowlit bar car. Teal lights flood the space and glint off the shelves of dark bottles on the far end of the wall. The bartender looks up from the cubes of ice he’s shaving and storing in a wooden bucket. The mailperson raises an envelope-stuffed hand and waves. “I have official correspondence for ‘X, the Twenty Fourth’ from the archival car, along with a personal letter addressed to ‘Octini’ from first class,” she says.

“Those are for me,” the bartender replies.

He returns his gaze to his knife. The mailperson sidles up to the corkboard while he’s preoccupied. A few empty patches are dotted between the sheets of paper. Any negative reviews she saw last time have been removed. Shocking, the bartender didn’t look like the sensitive type. She tacks on another testimonial (underneath the comment that declares him “easy on the eyes, hard on the stomach”) and hurries to the counter, gloved hand sliding two letters across the surface. His gaze stubbornly stays lowered.

“Thank you. Please go ahead and set them there,” he says.

He gestures further right, which the mailperson follows through with, but he continues shaving ice. She knits her fingers in front of her and idles. The thunks of the blade continue. The mailperson cranes her neck forward until her hair nearly brushes his hand, and the bartender finally notices. At his gaze, she gives a coy smile and curves her hand into an incomplete circle.

“Offer me something for my delivery?” she asks. She opens her mouth to mock drink from her non-existent bottle. She sees his thoughts tick before he reaches below the bar. His cleaver is set to the side with a clack.

“May I serve you a complimentary drink?” he asks.

“Oh, please do! I have no complaints, oh, you’re so generous,” she says. Her smile splits wide as she slings herself onto one of the barstools. The bartender takes out a can of beer. “Oh, oh, oh, hold on to that. I’d prefer something a bit more red?”

“Of course.”

The bartender stays standing where he is, but winding limbs clamber up the shelves behind him before plucking a bottle from the lower shelf. The tentacles grasp the bottle of wine, and a sucker latches onto the cork. With a pop, a fine mist of alcoholic relief escapes into the air. His hand steadies a wine glass as a tentacle tips the bottle. Two glugs and he stops. The bartender scrawls something down on a bright yellow slip with his spare hand.

Served letter carrier—

The mailperson stands on the bar around the stool’s legs, snatches the paper scrap, and sticks it under her cap. She tugs the hat tight around her skull.

“You don’t have to worry about recording me as a customer, Octini,” she says. She chugs the wine and hops off the stool. “You left all your mail by the cart near the door, right?”

The bartender’s hand fumbles a moment in the air. He returns his pencil to his sash.

“You’re correct. Please go ahead,” he says.

“Thank you for your understanding, and I’ll get out of your hair,” she replies. 

She follows the walls and snatches up the sheafs of paper and books left near her exit. All things considered, delivering letters to and from the bar car wasn’t such a bad deal. Her colleague was right that you could squeeze a drink out of the bartender (against the conductor’s employee intoxication ban) if his mood were good. The mailperson chews a mint as she flips through a notebook the bartender intended to mail. This must be the overdue package the archivist was grouching about. 

Shrimp cocktail. Snake wine. An old-fashioned on rocks. (The bartender sourced pebbles from the fish tank.) These are some of the unconventional offerings the bar car provides. I’m getting more fruit here than with the daily board on this train. Honest, this is the first time I’m seeing sullyberries outside of my hometowne, and considering it got wiped out six years ago with the spring-eastern sinkhole, I have not a clue how he got his hands on them. The bartender is too stingee to fess up about his sources no matter how many times I ask him, but he sure is generous with the drinking snacks. He serves a fine pisco sour, pairs nice with the berries.
—Testimonial from Mr. N

Octini rifles through a crate of peaches. Tentacles delve between bruised skins and come up with nothing. A customer taps his foot while polishing off his third perfectly peeled orange. His finger takes over the rhythm once his ankle starts aching.

“May I ask you to repeat your order, sir?” Octini asks.

“Pisco sour, like what Newman recommended?” the customer replies, his voice clipped.

“Please give me just a moment,” Octini says.

The bartender pulls himself off the ground and plucks alcohol off the shelves. The bottles are set on the counter with a clatter as his tentacles suction onto sheets of paper plastered on the corkboard. While skimming through the testimonials for keywords, he spins a jigger between his fingers and pours a portion of pisco into a cup. There was egg right? He passes off the sealed shaker tin to his tentacles and pulls a note off the laminated bar menu.

A liquor base. Garnish. Select a fitting glass. Refer to notebook for recipes.

He opens the drawer to his right. A few extra pencils roll around on top of a bright yellow square labeled NOTEBOOK - Daily journal + recipes. He squints at the empty drawer but continues shaking the pisco until frothy. He sets a cube of ice into a glass and pours in the drink. It’s nearly opaque, and the foam is a creamy yellow. He squeezes lime into the drink, then garnishes it with parsley. He sets the cup in front of the customer, who picks up the glass to take a swig.

“It’s… thick… ” the customer says. He looks a bit confused as he sips again. “It’s fine, but it’s not pisco sour. Where’s the sweet? Where’s the bitters? Why does it taste like egg?”


“It’s avant-garde,” Octini replies.
